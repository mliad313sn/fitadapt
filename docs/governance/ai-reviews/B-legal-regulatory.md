## 1. The founder's question: "a disclaimer signed during use to avoid any legal pursuit"

### 1.1 Short answer

**No click-to-accept or type-to-accept text can stop a user from suing, or remove liability for injury caused by the company's negligence, in any of the five target markets.** In the EU (France) and the UK, such a clause is void by statute or black-listed, and in the UK agreeing to it does not even count as accepting the risk. In Senegal, a contract clause cannot exclude liability for bodily harm. In the US it depends on the state: some states void pre-injury releases entirely, and nearly all refuse them for gross negligence or breach of safety statutes. Côte d'Ivoire is unconfirmed but very likely follows the same civil-law line.

A well-built acknowledgment still does a lot of useful work:
1. It proves the user was **warned**. That matters for failure-to-warn and defect arguments, and for showing the user was at fault in part (contributory fault / *faute de la victime*).
2. It proves the user **was informed and chose** the activity and its intensity. That supports the inherent-risk and assumption-of-risk defences where they exist, mainly in the US.
3. It shows the user **agreed to the terms that can lawfully apply**: service scope, "not medical advice", governing law, subscription terms.
4. It is evidence the insurer and the court will ask for.

The protection that actually works is the one the spec already names (`docs/specs/00-legal-framework.md`, preamble): a product that is safe by design (S1–S7), honest claims (L1), notices at the point of risk (L3), a defensibility log (L11), a limited company, and insurance. The Terms draft already includes the right carve-out ("Nothing in these terms limits or excludes our liability for death or personal injury caused by our negligence…"). **Keep it.** Taking it out would make the Terms *less* enforceable in the EU and UK, not more.

**Conclusion for the founder.** Present the screen as an *informed acknowledgment of risk and of how to use the app safely*, not as a "waiver" or "release". In the EU and UK, a text that suggests the user has given up rights they still have can itself be unfair or misleading, and it weakens the rest of the terms. A US-only express assumption-of-risk and release clause, limited to ordinary negligence and inherent risks, can be added **by US counsel per state**. It will not work in every state and never for gross negligence.

### 1.3 What makes an acceptance record strong evidence

Courts and regulators look at the same few things. Each is paired with the legal hook that makes it matter:

| Property | Why it matters | Hook |
|---|---|---|
| **Conspicuous and informed**: the text or a clear link is shown next to the action, in plain language, in the user's language | US assent test (*Meyer*, *Berman*); EU/UK transparency (Dir. 93/13 Art. 5); French-language rule for consumer documents | cases above; Loi 94-665 (unverified) |
| **Specific and unbundled**: separate actions for separate things (terms, risk acknowledgment, each consent) | GDPR consent must be specific and not bundled with the contract; tying is presumed not freely given (Art. 7(4), EDPB 05/2020) | [EDPB 05/2020](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/our-work-tools/our-documents/guidelines/guidelines-052020-consent-under-regulation-2016679_en) — verified (excerpt) |
| **Affirmative act**: an unticked box or a clearly labelled button, not scrolling or continuing | US "unambiguous manifestation"; GDPR "clear affirmative act" | as above |
| **Attributable**: tied to an identified account or pseudonym, with device and app build | Code civil 1366 ("duly identified"); ESIGN/UETA (US, unverified) | Art. 1366 |
| **Versioned and reproducible**: exact text, version, locale, jurisdiction, *and what the screen looked like* | Proves what was shown, not just that a button was tapped | — |
| **Integrity-protected and time-anchored** | Code civil 1366 ("integrity"); eIDAS Art. 41(2): a qualified electronic time stamp is presumed accurate as to date, time and integrity of the data | [eIDAS (EUR-Lex)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2014/910/oj/eng) — verified (excerpt) |
| **Re-accepted on material change, with notice and a way out** | Unilateral change without valid reason is on the Dir. 93/13 indicative list (Annex 1(j)); P2B Reg. Art. 3(2) needs ≥ 15 days' notice for business users (coaches) | 93/13 (unverified for 1(j)); [P2B](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2019/1150/oj/eng) — verified (excerpt) |
| **Repeated at the point of risk** | A warning shown at the moment of the risk is stronger evidence of a warning than a clause at sign-up; the defect assessment weighs presentation and warnings | PLD 2024/2853 (secondary) |
| **Kept long enough** | Evidence purged before the claim period ends is lost | Code civil 2226; LA 1980 s.11/11A; PLD 25-year latent long-stop |

### 1.4 What FitAdapt already does well (read in the code)

- `packages/legal/src/acceptance.ts`: append-only records with **document id, version, locale, jurisdiction, source, SHA-256 of the exact rendered text and time**. The server rejects a hash that does not match the text of that version (`checkAcceptance`). Material changes need re-acceptance. Acceptances count only in the jurisdiction variant where they were given.
- `apps/mobile/src/screens/onboarding/LegalScreens.tsx`: "I accept" for Terms and Privacy appears **only after the full text has been opened**. The exercise-risk acknowledgment has its own screen. The first workout is gated (`firstWorkoutGate`, fails closed).
- Point-of-risk notices (`notices.ts`) are recorded as shown and acknowledged, with a content hash. The defensibility log is hash-chained, append-only by trigger, and has a legal-hold export.
- The Terms keep the non-excludable carve-out and the consumer's mandatory home-law protections.

### 1.5 Concrete improvements (M20 `packages/legal` + M01 onboarding), strongest first

None of these claims immunity. They make the record stronger and the flow fairer, which is the defence.

1. **Replace the device locale as the source of jurisdiction.** Today `_layout.tsx` takes the jurisdiction from `locales[0].regionCode` (and the in-memory fallback uses `ZZ`). A French resident with an en-US phone gets the US variant, and the user never confirms it. Ask **"Where do you live?"** at onboarding, pre-filled from the locale, and store `jurisdictionSource: 'user_confirmed' | 'device_locale' | 'store_country'` in the acceptance record. Without this, the "right text for the right market" evidence is weak. (B2, B1)
2. **Record *how* assent was given, not only *what*.** Add to `AcceptanceRecord`: `presentation` (screen id and flow version, e.g. `onboarding.terms@3`), `assentMethod` (`button_after_open`, `checkbox`, …), `textOpened: true`, `appBuild`, `engineVersion`, and the server receipt time next to the device time (you already cap offline records at 30 days). Keep a **rendered screenshot or template of every legal screen per release** in the release archive, so the company can show exactly what the screen looked like (*Berman* turned on the screen layout). (B2)
3. **Make the exercise-risk acknowledgment specific.** Keep it on one screen (L2), but split it into 3–4 short statements, each with its own **unticked** checkbox and stable statement id, recorded one by one. For example: "exercise has risks including rare heart events"; "I will stop and seek help for chest pain, faintness…"; "I answered the health questions honestly and will update them"; "I can skip, change or stop at any time". Add one sentence: **"This does not limit your legal rights."** That sentence helps in the EU/UK and costs nothing. A *typed* confirmation (typing "I agree") adds friction and accessibility cost and **does not change** what the law allows to be excluded. I don't recommend it as the default. If counsel wants it for a US variant, use it only there. (B2; A6 for the dark-pattern check)
4. **Fix the health-consent contradiction.** The consent text says "Without it, … only offers the most cautious training", but `firstWorkoutGate` **blocks the first workout** without `consent.health` (documents registry: `requiredBeforeFirstWorkout`). Telling users something false about what refusal does undermines both the consent (informed) and the defence. It also raises GDPR Art. 7(4) (consent tied to the service). Pick one: either (a) offer a screening-free, lowest-intensity mode without health consent, which S1 already fails closed on, and keep the text; or (b) change the text and have B1 confirm a lawful basis for making it a condition. I lean to (a). (B1; M20 open question 1)
5. **Stop "accepting" the Privacy Policy.** In the EU and UK the privacy notice is information. Accepting it is not a lawful basis and looks like bundling. Change the action to "I have read the Privacy Policy" (record it as an *acknowledgment*), and keep consents separate in the M17 ledger (already done). (B1)
6. **Anchor the log externally with a qualified timestamp.** Once a day, send the global chain head hash to an **eIDAS qualified time-stamp** service (Art. 41(2) presumption), or at least to write-once storage the company's admins cannot edit. This closes the known gap that the table owner can disable triggers (M20 deviation 6) and meets the "integrity" condition of Code civil 1366. (B3/M19; PE)
7. **Re-show the risk acknowledgment when the risk changes**, not only when the text changes: at the 12-month re-screen, after an S3 lock is lifted, and when a special-population flag appears. Record it as a notice (`notice.acknowledged`), not as a new contract version. (B2 with A1)
8. **Re-acceptance flow must never trap the user.** When a user declines new Terms: keep the stop and seek-care screens, export and deletion, and cancellation with a pro-rata refund working. Give at least 30 days' notice for consumers and at least 15 days for coaches (P2B Art. 3(2)). (B2)
9. **Users aged 16–17.** A minor's acknowledgment is weaker evidence everywhere, and US releases by minors are generally voidable. The app is already right not to rely on a waiver here. Consider an optional parent/guardian information notice for 16–17 (not a gate), and keep S4 and other safety limits for them. (B2, B1)
10. **Do not call anything a "waiver", "release" or "décharge"** in the EU/UK/FR/SN/CI variants. Put any US release wording only in the `US` variant, per state, drafted by US counsel. (B2)
