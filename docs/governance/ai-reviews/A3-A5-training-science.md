### Changes I would make first (highest safety or trust impact first)

1. **M07 `epleyMaxReps` (12): apply the limit to *effective* reps (reps + RIR), not to performed reps only.** Today 12 reps at RIR 5 enters Epley as 17 reps, which is outside the range where rep-based 1RM equations have been shown to hold (LeSuer 1997 and Reynolds 2006: accuracy falls off above about 10 reps). A too-high e1RM leads to heavier first loads. S5 and the 0.9 first-session factor limit the harm, but the input should be bounded where it enters. *Condition-type change: code, not the value.*
2. **M06 `incline_push_up_low` (0.41) is mapped to the wrong Ebben variant.** In Ebben et al. (2011), about 41 % is the value for hands on a ~61 cm box, the *higher* incline. The app's "low incline" is hands on a bench or chair seat (~40–50 cm), which falls between Ebben's 30.5 cm value (~55 %) and his 61 cm value (~41 %). A value near **0.48** fits better. The same source reports the 30.5 cm hands-elevated value, which the config does not use at all.
3. **M03 `zone.moderate.rpeMin` (4) → 5, and `zone.light.rpeMax` 3 → 4.** WHO 2020 defines moderate relative intensity as about 5–6 on a 0–10 scale and vigorous as about 7–8. Counting RPE 4 minutes as "moderate" over-credits the WHO 150–300 min ledger. That is a trust and legal-copy issue (it would tell people they meet a WHO guideline when they may not), not an injury risk.
4. **M04 `forecast.horizonDays` (365) with `forecast.minSpanDays` (21): cap the extrapolation.** A straight line fitted to 3 weeks and projected 12 months ahead will overstate progress, because strength and skill gains slow down over time. Suggested caps: a horizon of ≤ 3× the observed span and ≤ 180 days, and a *prediction* interval rather than slope ± 1.28 SE only. This is an L1 expectation-setting issue ("never a promise").
5. **M03 `tabata.maxBlocks` (3) and `hiit.maxRounds` (10) for people who just crossed the 2-week HIIT prerequisite.** Tabata's original protocol was a single ~4-minute block, done by trained athletes. For novices I would start at 1 block (or ≤ 6 HIIT rounds) and add blocks or rounds only after several completed interval sessions without red pain or S3 events. Today the ceiling is the same on the first HIIT day as on the hundredth.

### Items where the current value may be unsafe

- **None is clearly unsafe on its own**, because S1 (RPE ≤ 7 while a flag is unresolved), S5 (+10 %/7 days), the RIR ≥ 2 stop on assessment tests and the 0.9 first-session factor all bound the prescriptions. The two with the most potential for harm if the guards were ever relaxed are:
  - the **unbounded effective reps in Epley** (change 1): it can overestimate e1RM, pushing loads in the heavy direction;
  - the **HIIT volume ceiling on the first HIIT sessions** (change 5): it is the largest jump in cardiovascular demand the app prescribes, and seat A1 should see it together with A3/A5.

## Constants (not configuration), for completeness

- **S5 `S5_MAX_INCREASE_FRACTION` 0.1, `S5_WINDOW_DAYS` 7.** These are constants, and I propose no change. For context: ACSM 2009 describes load increases of about 2–10 %, so a 10 % ceiling within 7 days sits at the top of that range. In code, `s5LoadCeiling` takes the *lowest* load in the window × 1.1 and rounds down to the equipment, which is the strict reading. The only side effect, a stall on implements with coarse steps, is covered in #26.
- **`ASSESSMENT_MIN_STOP_RIR` 2.** Consistent with ACSM 2026 (no need to train to failure) and with not running maximal tests.
